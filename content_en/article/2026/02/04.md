The capabilities of the high-flux SM-3 reactor allow production of a large number of transplutonium isotopes for industry, medicine and science.
The most valuable isotope is <sup>252</sup>Cf for compact neutron sources.
The large-scale production of <sup>252</sup>Cf is a long and multi-stage process.
The final stage is the irradiation of targets with heavy curium isotopes.
Berkelium-249 accumulates in curium targets as a byproduct with <sup>252</sup>Cf and has no value for practical use.
However, when berkelium and its decay product <sup>249</sup>Cf are irradiated in the reactor, additional amounts of <sup>252</sup>Cf can be obtained.
This work presents the results of computational and experimental studies on irradiation of mixture of <sup>249</sup>Bk and <sup>249</sup>Cf in the SM-3 reactor.
Three different types of reactor targets, containing aluminum oxide ceramic foam, quartz powder and copper tablets as a matrix were investigated.
Its neutron-physical and thermophysical characteristics, important for effectiveness of <sup>252</sup>Cf accumulation, were evaluated.
The main results of irradiated targets radiochemical processing are provided, including comparison of experimental/calculated <sup>249</sup>Cf yields and substantiation of optimal target matrix material for irradiation of milligram amounts of berkelium and californium in the SM-3 reactor.
