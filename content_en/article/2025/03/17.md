This article provides a chronological overview of the scientific research carried out by the IPPE using the BR-1 reactor, which was a unique facility for that time.
From 1955 to 1959 (with the exception of a brief period when the BR-2 reactor was operated at the IPPE in 1956–1957), the BR-1 was the only one experimental fast reactor in the USSR.
Its plutonium core served as a powerful source of fast neutrons for physical experiments.
The purpose of these experiments was to study the neutron propagation in materials of interest for fast reactors, as well as to verify and refine the nuclear-physical constants used in the calculations of fast neutron reactors.
The BR-1 reactor was used to measure the plutonium conversion ratio in fast reactors (up to 2.6 maximum).
Experiments on BR-1 have also shown that in the case of a concentrated plutonium core and water-uranium reflector the plutonium generation rate can reach an extremely high value – up to 5.5.
The role of resonance effects in the field of fast neutrons was revealed for the first time, fast neutron spectra were measured in media with high resolution for the first time, and a specially developed resonant filter method was used for measuring spectra in fast systems.
